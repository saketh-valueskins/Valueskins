# General Influencer & Creator Terminology

## 1. Content Creators vs Influencers

### Content Creator
- **Definition:** Anyone creating and publishing content (videos, photos, reels, posts, blogs, podcasts, etc.)
- **Follower Base:** May range from 0 to millions
- **Focus:** Quality and consistency of the work itself, not audience size
- **Monetization:** Directly through content quality, not necessarily through sponsorships
- **Example:** Photographer posting beautiful shots, filmmaker making short films, writer publishing articles

### Influencer
- **Definition:** A content creator with a significant, engaged follower base whose audience trusts their recommendations
- **Follower Base:** Typically 10K+ followers (varies by platform; micro-influencers start at 1K)
- **Focus:** Audience relationship and influence over their community
- **Monetization:** Primarily through sponsorships, brand collaborations, and paid promotions
- **Example:** TikToker with 500K followers, Instagram lifestyle creator with 100K engaged audience, YouTube tech reviewer with loyal subscribers

**Key Difference:** All influencers are content creators, but not all creators are influencers. An influencer's power comes from their audience's trust and willingness to act on their recommendations.

## 2. Digital Rights / BCA Rights (Brand Collaboration Agreement Rights)

Digital rights refer to a brand's permission to use, repurpose, and exploit a creator's content beyond the original delivery or post. These rights are **separate from content creation fees** and require additional negotiation and payment.

### Types of Digital Rights

#### A. **Reposting & Republishing Rights**
- Brand can repost creator's content on their own channels (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, etc.)
- Content appears under brand's handle, not creator's
- Includes ability to pin/feature the content
- May or may not include creator credit/tag
- **Example:** Brand reposts creator's unboxing video on brand's Instagram feed

#### B. **Advertising & Commercial Use Rights**
- Brand can use content in paid advertising campaigns
- Includes social media ads, Google ads, display ads
- Can be used in commercial contexts beyond organic social
- May include use in email campaigns, newsletters
- **Example:** Brand uses creator's review video in a TikTok ad campaign to reach 1M+ people

#### C. **Broadcast Rights**
- Permission to air content on TV, streaming platforms, or broadcast media
- Content shown on bigger screens and wider distribution
- Often requires higher payment than social-only rights
- May include cable TV, OTT platforms, cinema pre-rolls
- **Example:** Brand airs creator's testimonial on national TV commercial

#### D. **Derivative Works & Modification Rights**
- Brand can edit, remix, crop, or modify the content
- Includes adding voiceovers, subtitles, graphics, music
- Can combine creator's content with other creators' content
- Includes colorgrading changes, aspect ratio adjustments
- **Example:** Brand edits down creator's 60-second video into 15-second reels for different platforms

#### E. **Multi-Platform Usage Rights**
- Content can be used across multiple platforms beyond where it was originally created
- **Social Only:** Instagram, TikTok, Facebook only (most restrictive)
- **All Platforms:** Every platform including emerging ones, websites, apps
- **Example:** Creator posts on TikTok, brand uses same video on LinkedIn, YouTube, website, email

#### F. **Exclusive Content Rights**
- Creator cannot post similar content for competing brands during agreed period
- Brand has exclusive use of the content
- Prevents creator from working with direct competitors
- Time-limited (usually 30-90 days)
- **Example:** Fitness brand gets exclusive rights; creator cannot promote other fitness brands during exclusivity period

#### G. **Perpetual vs Time-Limited Rights**
- **Perpetual:** Brand owns content forever, no expiry
- **Time-Limited:** Brand can use for X days/months/years, then rights revert to creator
- Most common: 30, 60, 90, 120, 180 days or perpetual
- **Example:** Brand gets 90-day rights; after 90 days, brand must take down the content

#### H. **Geographic Restrictions**
- Content usage limited to specific countries or regions
- **Example:** Content can be used in India only, not globally

#### I. **Usage Rights (Days/Duration)**
- How long brand can display/use the content
- 30 days (social only) = brand can post for 1 month
- 90 days (all platforms) = brand can use everywhere for 3 months
- 180 days perpetual = 6 months then forever
- After expiry, content must be removed (unless perpetual)
- **Example:** Creator grants 60-day rights; after 60 days, brand removes all posts with that content

#### J. **Attribution & Credit Rights**
- Whether creator is credited/tagged when content is reused
- Creator name, handle, watermark visibility
- Can impact creator's personal brand
- **Example:** Brand can repost but must tag creator's handle

#### K. **Commercial vs Non-Commercial Rights**
- **Non-Commercial:** Content used for brand awareness only, no direct sales pitch
- **Commercial:** Content used to directly sell products, make revenue
- Commercial rights are more expensive
- **Example:** Non-commercial = awareness post; Commercial = "Buy now" sales ad

## 3. Ad Boost (Content Amplification)

### Definition
Ad boost is when a brand **pays a social media platform to amplify/promote** a creator's content to reach a larger audience than organic reach allows.

### How It Works
1. Creator publishes content (post, reel, video, etc.)
2. Brand negotiates ad boost as additional service
3. Brand pays platform (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, etc.) to show content to targeted users
4. Content reaches far more people than creator's followers alone
5. Results: More views, likes, comments, engagement

### Examples
- Creator's post gets 5K organic views → Brand pays ₹10K to boost → Reaches 100K+ people
- Creator posts TikTok → Brand sponsors TikTok ad to show it to 18-25 year old women in India
- YouTube video gets 10K views → Brand pays for YouTube Ads to get 500K views

### Difference from Organic Reach
- **Organic:** Only follower base sees it
- **Boosted:** Algorithmically targeted audience (paying users) see it
- Boost extends beyond follower network to cold audience
- Brand's budget determines reach

### When It's Negotiated
- Usually added to digital rights deals
- "Creator grants digital rights + brand will boost content on TikTok"
- Cost varies by platform, audience size, duration
- Separate from content creation fee

## Creator Considerations When Selling Digital Rights

- **Always negotiate separately** from content fee
- **Specify exactly** which content pieces get rights (which reels, which stories)
- **Set time limits** unless perpetual rights are significantly higher paid
- **Understand modification rights** if brand can edit your content
- **Check exclusivity terms** to avoid competing work
- **Request credit/attribution** if brand reposts
- **Keep audit trail** of all agreements on ValueSkins (immutable records provided)
